# Keep sets: a window left keeps its photo and neighbours

What: `spikes/rust/bench/session-bench.py` with the full-screen setup fixed (the first window put in full screen
only when its `AXFullScreen` is false, docs/bench/2026-10-25-restore-and-rereads.md §2): 5 full-screen windows
restored from Last Session, 20 fast flips during the restore, END/HOME, then per window ⌃← + 4 flips + Enter to the
browser + Enter back. Counted from the `LOUPE_DEBUG` log: `other windows −N (M MB)` (what eviction took from inactive
windows over the run, `CacheStats.evictedInactive`), `kept N MB` (what the inactive windows' keep sets hold,
`CacheStats.keptForOtherWindows`) and the state each window is found in on the first ⌃← to it (`activate N state=…`).

Machine: MacBook Pro M4 Max (10P + 4E), 36 GB, macOS 27.0.1; release builds (arm64); `cache-memory` 4 GiB (the
default here), `keep-share` 50. Data: one 147 MP JPEG XL in each of `photo-share/benchmark/session-{1..5}`
(PC share, Ethernet). The machine was loaded by other work (load average 8–11) during all runs.

Sides: **before** = `40a6cd9` (B + C, docs/bench/2026-10-25-restore-and-rereads.md); **keep sets** = window-ownership
plan tasks 1–3 (`CacheOwners.keepSets`, `keep-share`, the keep sets planned in `ImageCache` in the `.keep` class).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before k1 | −67 (9241 MB) | – | bytes, bytes, bytes | 59 / 200 ms | 166 % |

Findings:

- What eviction takes from the other windows goes from 9.2–9.4 GB to 5.7–6.6 GB of 4 GiB × the run; the count of
  entries rises (−65…−121 against −66…−67) because they are now the far neighbours' small previews, not the photos on
  screen. About 2 GB (`keep-share` 50 % of 4 GiB) stays held for the windows not in front.
- A window switched to was found with only its file before (`state=bytes`: its pyramid had been evicted, every
  switch decoded the 147 MP photo again); with keep sets 8 of 12 arrivals find their preview resident. The 4 at
  `bytes` are windows whose keep set had not been loaded yet: the restored windows load theirs behind the front
  window, only while it dwells with its preview up.
- The main-thread probe (an Apple Event round trip): no stall > 250 ms in 5 of 6 runs; k2 had two (325, 344 ms at
  19.8 and 22.1 s) while the Loupe log shows its display-link callbacks every 16 ms through both, so the main thread
  kept turning; not reproduced in k3 and k4. CPU of the run 126–159 % against 166 %.
- Not measured here: Intel, a NAS share, `rereads` (task 5 of the plan counts them).

## Reads past half finished, re-reads counted (plan task 5)

- 6 of 6 arrivals find their preview (keep sets alone: 8 of 12); what is taken from the other windows 5.2–5.5 GB
  (5.7–6.6). Four files are read again in a run (the counter is new, so there is no figure before).
- The fast flips during the restore (20 at 0.15 s while five full-screen windows enter their Spaces) scatter on every
  build — navigate p50 159–848 ms, sharp p50 279–6414 ms — and are not compared.
- `finishReadAbove` is the spec's starting value: on this PC share a 147 MP file reads in 0.1–0.4 s, so few reads
  are caught half-way; the NAS run of the window-ownership bench is to confirm it.

## The stamp check on a return stays the current photo's (plan task 6 not done)

Task 6 would have queued the stamp check of a window switched back to, when its photo is already sharp, as an
ordinary viewer read rather than the current photo's, so that it pauses no other read. What it pauses for is one
`stat`: measured here (`os.stat` on 20 files each, two rounds) 16 ms p50, 49 ms p90, 108 ms max on the NAS cold and
0 ms once the client has the attributes; 0.5 ms on the PC share. Queued as an ordinary read it would wait behind a
neighbour or keep read holding that thread, seconds on a slow share, with the cache's activity `.current` all along
(the thumbnails held back): the case `stampCheckOfTheCurrentPhotoIsCurrent` was written for. Left as it is.

## A closed window frees at once (plan task 8)

What: three windows (`open-in=window`) on one 147 MP JPEG XL each (`PIR020_s0{1..3}_fullres`, PC share), 6 s to
settle, then ⌘W on the front window twice; the process's physical footprint (`footprint -p`, system and graphics
memory) before and every 0.5 s for 5 s after. The script (scratchpad `close-bench.py`, to be moved to `scripts/` with
the other benches) sends keystrokes, and in one run the second ⌘W did not land: the log's `viewer window freed`
lines say how many closed.

| Run | Build | Windows closed | Before | 0.5 s after | 5 s after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | before (tasks 1–5) | 2 | 6.23 GB | 5.87 GB | 5.86 GB |
| 2 | before | 2 | 6.15 GB | 5.79 GB | 5.85 GB |
| 1 | close release | 2 | 6.06 GB | 3.12 GB | 3.68 GB |
| 2 | close release | 1 | 6.15 GB | 4.66 GB | 5.47 GB |

Before, a closed window's photos stayed until some other window needed the room. Now what only it listed goes at
once (`ImageCache.unregister`) and the view lets its image go (`ViewerView.teardown`): 2.9 GB back in half a second
for two windows, 1.5 GB for one. Afterwards the window left fills the room it got with its own plan (its neighbours,
up to `cache-memory`), which is the climb in the last column.
